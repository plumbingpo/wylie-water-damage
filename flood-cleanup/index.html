In nearly every case the water falls into Category 2, known as gray water, or Category 3, known as black water, which means contaminants, bacteria and other hazards that are a real health risk. Warning Signs for Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Flooring that squelches when you walk on it and walls damp to the touch
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Seepage at door thresholds, window wells or cracks in the foundation
  • Any amount of water sitting in a room, garage or basement
  • Basement flooding after the sump pump quit in a downpour
  • Rainwater getting inside through a roof or wall damaged by a storm
  • Water collecting along the foundation because the yard is saturated

Why Flood Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. Floodwater carries bacteria, sewage, chemicals and other contaminants that are serious health hazards. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Add downspout extensions so roof water empties well away from the foundation
  • Have a backwater valve fitted to the sewer line so sewage cannot flow back inside
  • A battery-powered backup sump pump keeps water moving if the electricity fails in a storm
  • Store valuables and important papers off the basement floor in waterproof containers
  • Clear leaves and debris from storm drains and gutters, which lets water drain the way it should
  • Check that grading on all sides of the house carries water away from the foundation

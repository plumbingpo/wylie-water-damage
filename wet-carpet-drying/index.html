Wet Carpet Drying: What Wylie Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. Our team in Wylie extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

Warning Signs

  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Soaked Pad Underneath

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

Furniture Marks

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

What We Do

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

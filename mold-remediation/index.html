Maintenance Tips for Mold Remediation

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Laundry rooms, baths and kitchens all need working exhaust ventilation that is sized for the space
  • Repair any leak inside 24 hours, since 24 to 48 hours of moisture is all mold requires to begin
  • Aim for 30 to 50% indoor humidity and never let it stay above 60%, running dehumidifiers and ventilating as needed
  • Mold in ductwork is prevented by servicing the heating and cooling system and replacing its filters on schedule
  • Keep gutters clean so rainwater does not seep in along the foundation
  • A vapor barrier is worth considering in a crawl space, which also needs correct ventilation

Why Professional Mold Remediation Matters

This is not a job to take on yourself. Without containment, scrubbing or tearing out moldy material releases spores by the millions, potentially spreading contamination through the house and making the health exposure worse than it was. Professional mold remediation uses negative air pressure containment, HEPA-filtered air scrubbers and personal protective equipment so spores are captured and contained during removal.

A professional job goes after the moisture source too, because that is why the mold grew in the first place. If the moisture is left alone, taking away what you can see only buys time, and regrowth often shows up within weeks. Certified remediation technicians have the training and equipment to identify hidden moisture sources, dry affected areas properly and put solutions in place that prevent recurrence.

Signs It Is Time for Mold Remediation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Closets, bathrooms or other damp, enclosed rooms that always smell musty
  • Mold or mildew that comes back soon after you clean it
  • Warping, blistered paint or wallpaper lifting off the wall, all clues to moisture you cannot see
  • Water damage not long ago that was left to dry without professional equipment
  • Recurring respiratory symptoms, allergies or headaches that improve when you are away from home
  • Long-running hidden leaks, for example at a dishwasher line, sink drain or roof flashing
  • Discolored or darkened spots near HVAC vents, across ceilings or on walls

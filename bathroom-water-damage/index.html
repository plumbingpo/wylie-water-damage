Bathroom Water Damage in Wylie

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. Our Wylie team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
Our Process

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Damage Downstairs

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Trapped Steam

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

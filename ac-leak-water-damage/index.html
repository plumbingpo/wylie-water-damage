AC Leak Water Damage in Wylie: What to Know

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Drain pans that sit unevenly or have corroded and split.
  • Loose or separated fittings on the condensate pipe that drip where nobody can see.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • The drain pipe for condensate blocked by a buildup of dust and algae.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Mold Near the Return

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Ceilings Under the Unit

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Insulation Around the Unit

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

The Platform and Closet Walls

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Measure the Spread

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Extract and Open Up

Our extractors pull water out of the pan, the wood platform and any carpet. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Set Drying Equipment

We monitor readings throughout the several days, usually 3 to 5, that the fans and dehumidifiers need. Machines are positioned to leave the HVAC technician a clear path to the unit.

4

Clean and Restore

Since condensate left standing for days is seldom clean, we apply an antimicrobial to the surfaces it touched. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.

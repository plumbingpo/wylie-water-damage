Kitchen Water Damage Help in Wylie

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. For homeowners in Wylie, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Signs to Look For

  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • The toe kick boards have puffed up, turned dark, or come loose
Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Layers of Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

Under the Base Cabinets

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Sink Drain Leaks

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry in Place

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Remove Only Damage

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

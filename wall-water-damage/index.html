Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Wylie Homeowners Should Know

Think of drywall as a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Warning Signs

  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Trim along the floor that has puffed up, twisted or come loose
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Cut or Ventilate

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Walls You Cannot See

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Insulation Holding Water

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

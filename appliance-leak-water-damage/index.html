Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Wylie

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Wylie, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Document What FailedPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
  • Worn dishwasher door gaskets, cracked drain hoses or a failed pump seal.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Wall Bases and Trim

Drywall edges and baseboards pull up water that stands on a kitchen or laundry room floor. The wall behind the appliance is often the wettest because nobody could see it.

Kitchen Cabinets

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. Panels swell and come apart, and a wet cabinet box can keep the wall behind it damp for weeks.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Whether the water was clean supply water or gray discharge that needs sanitizing.
  • Whether this was a sudden flood or slow seepage, and which machine it came from.
  • Presence of mold, which adds removal work in front of the drying.
  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.

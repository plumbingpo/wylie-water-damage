Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

The structural drying technicians at Wylie Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

How We Handle Structural Drying

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

Using moisture meters, thermal imaging and hygrometers, we create a comprehensive moisture map of all affected areas. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Setting the Equipment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. You receive updates on the drying timeline.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Tell your restoration technician immediately if you notice new wet areas or odors during drying
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted

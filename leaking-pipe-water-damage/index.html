Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Wylie

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. Our team in Wylie finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. That is our part of the job. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the Water SupplyFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Check the Water MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Have the Area MeteredHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Read your policy and talk to your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Pinhole corrosion in older copper lines that slowly eats through the pipe wall.
  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
  • A crimp ring or solder joint that gives out inside a ceiling or wall cavity where nobody can see it.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Hidden Mold Growth

A dark, damp wall cavity is where mold does best. With a slow leak it often covers the back of the drywall before a single spot shows on the painted side.

Decayed Wall Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Damaged Flooring

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. Laminate seams swell, hardwood boards cup, and the glue holding tile or vinyl releases.

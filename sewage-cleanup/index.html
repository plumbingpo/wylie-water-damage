A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Across Wylie, Wylie Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
